Do Not Imitate, Reinforce

Iterative Classification via Belief Refinement

Mahdi Kallel1, Johannes Tölle2, Ahmed Hendawy3, Carlo D'Eramo1

1Center for Artificial Intelligence and Data Science, University of Würzburg 2Automation and Robotics Research Group, SnT, University of Luxembourg 3Department of Computer Science, Technical University of Darmstadt

Paper OpenReview Code
A classifier rewarded for improving its prediction, not for copying the label.

TL;DR

Reinforced Iterative Classification (RIC) casts classification as a Markov decision process in which a recurrent actor iteratively refines a predictive distribution and is rewarded for the increase in log probability assigned to the label; a critic estimates the value of continued refinement and supplies a stopping rule at inference. In the deterministic limit RIC targets the same prediction as cross-entropy, but it reaches that target along a different path: accuracy rises ahead of confidence. Across ten image classification benchmarks, including medical images, RIC-SPO (RIC trained with Simple Policy Optimization) achieves a better balance between accuracy and calibration than single-pass and adaptive supervised baselines.

How it works

Start from a uniform belief

Each labeled example is its own small episode. The state holds the input, the current prediction, and a latent state, and the prediction starts from the uniform prior over the $K$ classes.

$$ s_0 = (x, a_0, \tau_0), \qquad a_0 = [1/K, \dots, 1/K], \qquad \tau_0 = \mathbf{0} $$

Refine the class distribution

A recurrent backbone updates the latent state from the input and the previous prediction. A Dirichlet policy head then samples the next class distribution on the simplex, and a value head predicts how much refinement remains useful.

$$ \tau_{t+1} = f_\theta(x, \tau_t, a_t), \qquad a_{t+1} \sim \mathrm{Dir}(c\mu) $$

Discounting trains every refinement depth

Reward the gain in label log-probability. The rewards telescope, so the discounted return scores the prediction at a random, geometrically distributed depth $N$: every step has to be a good answer, which makes RIC an anytime classifier.

$$ r_t = \log a_{t,y} - \log a_{t-1,y}, \qquad \mathbb{E}\Big[\textstyle\sum_{t\ge1}\gamma^{t-1} r_t\Big] = \mathbb{E}_{N\sim\mathrm{Geom}(1-\gamma)}\big[\log a_{N,y}\big] + \log K $$

The critic measures the improvement left

Measure the error $L_t = \mathrm{KL}(q\,\Vert\,a_t)$ to the true conditional $q$. Label ambiguity cancels, so the value tracks only reducible error: refinement continues while $V(s_t) > 0$ and stops once $V(s_t) \le 0$.

$$ V^\pi(s_t) = L_t - \mathbb{E}_{N\sim\mathrm{Geom}(1-\gamma),\,\pi}\!\left[L_{t+N} \mid s_t\right] $$

Results

  • SL’s validation return declines while RIC’s holds.
  • Policy-gradient updates are noisier, so RIC-SPO fits the training set more gradually, yet at matched training accuracy it generalizes better on CIFAR-10, Galaxy10 and ImageWoof.
  • At every noise level, RIC ends training with lower ECE than SL.
  • Cross-entropy ties confidence to fitting, so SL is near certain within tens of epochs. In RIC, accuracy rises ahead of confidence, and calibration emerges as confidence catches up.
  • With noisy labels the target itself is less certain, so RIC’s confidence settles lower and mislabeled examples keep moderate probabilities.
  • SL is overconfident in every bin. RIC is mildly underconfident in most.
  • On ImageWoof, RIC-SPO raises accuracy from 82.1% to 85.3% and more than halves ECE, from 0.085 to 0.038.
  • Across ten datasets, the deterministic and stochastic readouts form the Pareto front. On medical imaging, stochastic RIC is best or tied on ECE for 5 of 5 MedMNIST subsets, deterministic RIC on accuracy for 4 of 5.
  • ACT and PonderNet refine recurrently too, yet their calibration tracks SL’s.
  • Easy images saturate fast. Hard ones stop before reward turns negative.
  • Inputs that end up correctly classified receive more refinement steps than those that end up wrong.
  • Against vine estimates of the true future return, the critic makes the right stop-or-continue call on 69 to 87% of states, erring toward stopping early.

Final remarks

  • At deployment, RIC-SPO offers a choice: deterministic rollouts for accuracy, stochastic rollouts for calibration.
  • Better reinforcement learning algorithms can improve RIC further while preserving the training dynamics observed here.
  • Temperature scaling and label smoothing are orthogonal to RIC and can be combined with it.
