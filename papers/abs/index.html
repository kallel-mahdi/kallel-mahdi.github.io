Augmented Bayesian Policy Search

1Center for Artificial Intelligence and Data Science, University of Würzburg 2Univ. Lille, Inria, CNRS, Centrale Lille, UMR 9189 – CRIStAL 3Department of Computer Science, TU Darmstadt 4Hessian Center for Artificial Intelligence (hessian.AI)

What if Bayesian policy search knew it was solving an MDP?

TL;DR

Deterministic policies are often preferred on physical systems, yet exploration in reinforcement learning is largely performed by stochastic policies. First-order Bayesian optimization explores with deterministic policies through a probabilistic model of the return and its gradient, but treats policy search as a black box. Augmented Bayesian Search (ABS) uses the performance difference lemma to give that model a mean function built from the action-value function. The posterior gradient then augments Bayesian optimization with the deterministic policy gradient, and the same lemma gives a criterion to validate critics on any rollout. ABS outperforms earlier Bayesian optimization methods, with the widest margin on high-dimensional policies.

How it works

The derivative of a GP is a GP

Model the policy return as a Gaussian process over the policy parameters. Given a central point $\theta$ and the returns $Y$ observed at points $X$, the gradient at $\theta$ has a Gaussian posterior $\mathcal{N}(\mu_\theta, \Sigma_\theta)$.

$$ \begin{aligned} \mu_\theta &= \nabla_\theta m(\theta) + \nabla_\theta K(\theta, X)\,K(X, X)^{-1}\big(Y - m(X)\big), \\ \Sigma_\theta &= \nabla_\theta K(\theta, \theta) - \nabla_\theta K(\theta, X)\,K(X, X)^{-1}\,\nabla_\theta K(X, \theta) \end{aligned} $$

Compare any policy to the current one

The return of any policy $\pi_x$ can be written relative to the current policy $\pi_\theta$, through the advantage of $\pi_\theta$ under the states that $\pi_x$ visits.

$$ J(\pi_x) = J(\pi_\theta) + \mathbb{E}_{s \sim d^{\pi_x}}\big[A^{\pi_\theta}(s, \pi_x(s))\big] $$

The critic becomes the prior

Roll out the central point $\theta$, collect $\widehat{J}(\pi_\theta)$ and $\widehat{d}^{\pi_\theta}$, and train a critic $\widehat{Q}^{\pi_\theta}$. Evaluating the lemma on the states of $\pi_\theta$ gives a good prior for the return of any point $x$. The mean of the gradient posterior is then the deterministic policy gradient, corrected where this prior misses the observed returns.

$$ \widehat{m}_\phi(x) = \widehat{J}(\pi_\theta) + \mathbb{E}_{s \sim \widehat{d}^{\pi_\theta}}\big[\widehat{A}^{\pi_\theta}_\phi(s, \pi_x(s))\big] $$

The lemma gives a criterion to validate critics

Rolling out any point $x$ gives its return $\widehat{J}(\pi_x)$ and its states $\widehat{d}^{\pi_x}$. On those states the lemma predicts the return from the critic, so the prediction error measures the critic on trajectories other than those of $\pi_\theta$. ABS weights an ensemble of critics by this criterion.

$$ \begin{aligned} \widetilde{m}_\phi(x) &= \widehat{J}(\pi_\theta) + \mathbb{E}_{s \sim \widehat{d}^{\pi_x}}\big[\widehat{A}^{\pi_\theta}_\phi(s, \pi_x(s))\big], \\ \text{error}_\phi(x) &= \big(\widetilde{m}_\phi(x) - \widehat{J}(\pi_x)\big)^2 \end{aligned} $$

Results

  • The next query lies in the direction of the gradient posterior.
  • The maximum of the acquisition function follows the posterior mean $\mu_\theta$ (violet), which corrects the mean-function gradient $\nabla_\theta \widehat{m}_\phi$ (pink) when the mean function does not fit the observations.
  • Acquisitions stay in the immediate vicinity of $\theta$, which keeps $\Vert z - \theta \Vert$ small and with it the residual term of the decomposition behind the mean function.
  • ABS outperforms MPD, and pulls ahead of ARS on Walker2d and Ant.
  • On low-dimensional tasks ABS is competitive with MPD, the state of the art in local Bayesian optimization, and with Augmented Random Search (ARS).
  • In high dimensions, ARS and MPD struggle to find good ascent directions, while the advantage mean function focuses the search on promising regions of the parameter space.
  • The best critic explains about half of the variance of the observed returns.
  • The validation score stays positive. The test score on new acquisition points is often positive and sometimes negative, which is enough: the mean function only has to identify one good ascent direction at a time.
  • The two scores are positively correlated, and validation acts as an upper bound on test, as in supervised learning.
  • Softmax weighting with resets beats every other ensemble.
  • An ensemble outperforms a single critic, and weighting critics by their validation score outperforms plain averaging, especially early on, when transition data is scarce.
  • Resetting the worst critic helps most together with adaptive weighting, since the validation score detects a freshly reset critic and ignores its predictions.

Final remarks

  • The advantage mean function acts like a first-order Taylor expansion of the policy return, bridging Bayesian optimization and reinforcement learning.
  • ABS explores with deterministic policies, learns effectively in an episodic setting, and lets one trade compute for learning speed.
